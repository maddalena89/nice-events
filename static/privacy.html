Privacy & legal

The short version: this is a small free site with no ads and no trackers. It asks for your email in two places and uses it for exactly the thing it said it would. Nothing is sold, nothing is shared with advertisers, and you can have any of it deleted by asking.

Two things to confirm before this goes live privacy@whatsonnice.com must actually deliver somewhere you read — set up the forwarding rule first, or this page promises a contact route that doesn't exist. And check which region your Supabase project sits in (Settings → General); if it is not the EU, correct the line in section 4. Then delete this box.

1 · Who is behind this site

What's on in Nice is published by an individual, not a company, as a personal non-commercial project. French law (the LCEN) lets a private individual publishing a non-professional site stay anonymous to the public, provided the host holds their full identity — which is the case here.

Host: GitHub, Inc. (GitHub Pages), 88 Colin P. Kelly Jr. Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, United States. DNS and caching by Cloudflare, Inc.

Contact for anything on this page: privacy@whatsonnice.com

This changes the day money arrives Under the LCEN, a site carrying advertising, sponsorship, affiliate links or any commercial activity is no longer a personal site. At that point full legal mentions become mandatory: name, status, address, SIREN once registered, and the publication director. So if sponsored listings ever appear here, this section has to be rewritten first, not afterwards.

3 · What never happens

  • Your email is never displayed on the site, in the feed, or in any export.
  • Nothing is ever sold, rented or handed to advertisers or data brokers.
  • There are no advertising cookies, no tracking pixels, no social widgets, and no consent banner — because there is nothing to consent to.
  • No profiling, no automated decisions about you, no attempt to work out who you are across sites.
  • You are never added to the weekly email because you submitted an event or reported a bug. Those are separate things and stay separate.

4 · Who else touches the data

A handful of suppliers, each doing one job on instruction and nothing else:

  • Supabase — stores submissions and messages. Project hosted in the EU.
  • Resend — sends the confirmation and weekly emails. United States.
  • Anthropic — the model that reads poster photos. United States. The image is processed for the request and not retained.
  • GitHub Pages — serves the page itself. United States.
  • Cloudflare — DNS, caching and the cookieless visit counter.

Where a supplier is outside the EU, the transfer relies on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses in that supplier's terms.

5 · Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask, at any time, to: see what is held about you; correct it; delete it; restrict or object to its use; get a copy in a portable form; and withdraw consent for the weekly email — which you can also do in one click from the unsubscribe link at the bottom of every single one.

Email privacy@whatsonnice.com and it will be handled within a month, usually the same week. No form, no justification needed.

If you are not happy with how it was handled, you can complain to the CNIL, the French data protection authority: cnil.fr.

6 · Are you a source? Getting your events removed

This site gathers publicly listed events from tourist offices, museums, city agendas and specialist calendars, tidies them, and always links back to whoever is actually putting the event on. It is meant to send people to you, not to replace you.

If you run one of those sites or venues and would rather not be included, just say so and you are out — same day, no argument, no explanation required. Email privacy@whatsonnice.com or use the removal link on the home page. The same goes for a single event, a single venue, or everything from one source.
